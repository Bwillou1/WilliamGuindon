## Part 01 The story, told simply

In Blainville, in the Laurentians north of Montreal, there is a large swamp called Grande Tourbière
(Great Peat Bog). A bog is a spongy wetland where dead vegetation accumulates without completely
rotting. These environments filter water, store carbon and shelter species that are found nowhere
else.

Next door is a company called Stablex. Its job: to receive dangerous industrial waste – sludge
filled with heavy metals, for example – to mix it with cement to “freeze” it, then to bury it in
large airtight pits called cells. Its first five cells reach saturation. He needs a sixth.

The land coveted for this cell no. 6 belonged to the Ville de Blainville (City of Blainville), which
refused to cede it. In March 2025, the Quebec government resolved the problem in an expeditious
manner: it passed a special law, Loi 93 (Bill 93), passed overnight under a gag order (an emergency
procedure that cuts short the debate). This law expropriates the City, pays it $17 million,
transfers the land to Stablex — and in the process forecloses most recourses before the courts. The
City and the Metropolitan Community of Montreal tried to challenge it: they lost in the Superior
Court on April 2, 2025, then in the Court of Appeal on April 16, 2025.

The machines have arrived. The trees have fallen. It was at this moment that William Guindon, a
citizen of Blainville then aged 14 at the start of his efforts (now 15 years old), opened an
unprecedented legal front: rather than attacking Quebec or Stablex – two avenues blocked by the Loi
93 – he questioned the direct responsibility of Ottawa.

His legal reasoning is implacable:

Quebec can legislate on the transfer of land, but there are Canadian federal laws that protect fish
habitat, migratory birds and species at risk across the country, including on private or municipal
land. These laws fall under the exclusive jurisdiction of Ottawa. And Ottawa remained completely
silent.

The strength of the case lies in this demonstration: it does not criticize the federal government
for having poorly exercised its discretionary power, but for never having exercised it. No known
federal inspections, no permits issued or refused under the Fisheries Act or the Species at Risk
Act, no reasoned decisions.

By mobilizing the international mechanism of chapter 24 of the ACEUM (SEM process) from May 1, 2026,
then by filing on July 16, 2026 a consolidated communication of exceptional density - 15-page brief,
102-page analytical annex and 222 evidentiary documents -, he has built a file that stands the test
of international filtering.

On August 17, 2026, the Commission de coopération environnementale (Commission for Environmental
Cooperation) made a historic determination: the communication met all of the admissibility criteria
of the North American treaty. The Government of Canada is legally required to respond by October 16,
2026.

## Part 03 The place: three superimposed layers

### 3.1 — The Grande Tourbière de Blainville
A wetland complex which, according to provincial inspection reports on file, includes “a marsh, a
swamp, shallow water and a peat bog”. There we find plants indicative of wetlands — pitcher plant (a
carnivorous plant), cotton grass — growing on permanent sphagnum mats. The Bureau d'audiences
publiques sur l'environnement (BAPE), in its report No. 371, highlighted the “exceptional ecological
value” of the sector and advised the government against authorizing the project.

A geological detail that matters enormously. The area is underlain by thick deposits of clay from
the Champlain Sea — a sea that covered the St. Lawrence Valley about 10,000 years ago. These clays,
15 meters thick on the surface and up to 50 meters deep, are subject to “deep ravinement”: the water
creates tree-shaped networks. In practical terms, this means that any sediment or contaminant that
reaches the ground is transported quickly and far to waterways. It is not a ground that holds back;
it is a ground that leads.

### 3.2 — Cell No. 6
The land in question is lot 6 695 053 of the Quebec land register. According to media coverage and
BAPE (Québec Bureau of Public Environmental Hearings), it includes approximately 9 hectares of
wetlands and 58 hectares of woodlands. According to the proponent's impact study, the 52-hectare
study area contains 29.2 hectares of wetlands (56% of the surface area), including a 19.1-hectare
tree swamp. The planned backfilling would cover 278,000 m², or 27.8 hectares. (These different
figures relate to different perimeters — see Part 9.)

The agreement in principle signed on March 11, 2020 between the City and the developer provides for
24 hours a day, 7 days a week operation. The new site would allow Stablex to operate for around
forty more years, until around 2065.

### 3.3 — What was there before: Camp Bouchard
This is perhaps the most surprising element of the file. From 1942 to 1980, this same land was home
to Camp Bouchard, a munitions production plant and explosives depot operated by the Canadian
Department of National Defense. In other words: the soil was first contaminated by the federal
government itself.

The site still appears on the Federal Contaminated Sites Inventory (number 06875001), maintained by
the Treasury Board Secretariat. According to the data cited in the complaint:
- Identified contaminants: metals, metalloids and organometallics in groundwater and soil
- File status: “Active” — it is not closed
- Management strategy: “Additional assessment” — work is not completed
- Expenditures in 2025-2026: $0 on evaluation, $0 on restoration, $0 on monitoring
- Area restored in 2025-2026: 0 hectare
- Historical total expenditures: approximately $147,000 for a 4-hectare military site operated for 38
-   years

Stablex itself publicly attributes part of the metals detected to this previous military use. The
author draws two formidable questions from this: (1) did the Ministry of Defense decontaminate
before ceding the land? A freedom of information request reportedly found no documentation of
decontamination work. (2) If the metals found in the stream come in part from the former federal
military site, why has Ottawa never investigated?

## Part 04 The mechanism, from A to Z

### 4.1 — Where does this tool come from
In 1994, when signing NAFTA, the three countries feared that one state would be tempted to attract
factories by turning a blind eye to pollution. They therefore created, in parallel, an environmental
agreement equipped with a citizen valve. When NAFTA was replaced by ACEUM on July 1, 2020, this
valve was retained and written directly into the trade treaty, in articles 24.27 and 24.28.

Curiosity of vocabulary: the official French text of ACEUM speaks of “observations on questions of
application”. The Commission continues to use the word “communications”. Both mean exactly the same
thing: a citizen complaint. The acronym SEM comes from the English Submissions on Enforcement
Matters.

### 4.2 — First question: what is an “environmental law”?
All the legal sorting of the decision follows from this definition. Here is the official text, in
article 24.1 of ACEUM:
> “environmental law means a law or regulation of a Party, or one of its provisions […] the primary
> object of which is the protection of the environment, or the prevention of a danger to health or
> human life, by, as the case may be: a) the prevention, reduction or control of the discharge […] of
> pollutants or contaminants; (b) the control of dangerous or toxic chemicals, substances, materials
> or waste […]; (c) the protection or conservation of wild flora and fauna, including endangered
> species, their habitat and specially protected natural areas […]” “law or regulation means […] for
> Canada, an Act of the Parliament of Canada or a regulation made under an Act of the Parliament of
> Canada which may be enforced by action of the central government. »

For an article of law to be retained by the CCE (Commission for Environmental Cooperation), it must
meet three strict conditions: (1) have as its primary purpose the protection of the environment or
human health; (2) be a federal law or regulation passed in Ottawa; (3) fall under the application of
the federal government. It is this surgical filter that allowed the Secretariat to focus the
investigation on the four federal ecological pillars invoked in the communication (Fisheries Act,
SARA, LCOM, CEPA), while ruling out provisions of a purely commercial or general constitutional
nature.

### 4.4 — The four criteria of opportunity (article 24.27(3))
Second filter: does the complaint merit disturbing a government?
  · “if it is alleged that harm has been suffered by the person making the observations”
  · Be personally affected. The author invokes environmental damage (his living environment), health damage (exposure) and intergenerational damage (he will inherit the site).
  · “if the observations […] raise questions for which in-depth study would be conducive to achieving the objectives of this chapter”
  · Would digging into the subject do anything useful for North America?
  · “if the private remedies provided for by the law of the Party have been exercised”
  · Have first tried the remedies available at home — or credibly explain why it was impossible.
  · “if the observations are not drawn exclusively from information from mass media”
  · Don't settle for a pile of newspaper clippings. We need official documents, studies, data.

An important counterbalance, that said: the Secretariat also recalls that these criteria “must not
constitute an insurmountable procedural obstacle” and must be interpreted in the broad sense. He
cites three precedents in this sense (Loggerhead Turtle 2021, Fairview Terminal 2021, Gulf of
California Porpoise 2021). The door is narrow, but we must not barricade it.

### 4.5 — Deadlines, calculated to the nearest day
The Secretariat explains in a footnote how it counts: in calendar days; the day of departure is not
counted, the day of arrival is; if the deadline falls on a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday, it
slides to the next working day; a document received on a non-working day is deemed received on the
following working day.

### 4.6 — The complete journey, step by step
To compile the factual record, the Secretariat may draw on any public information, contributions
from interested persons, opinions of independent experts and the work of the Joint Public Advisory
Committee. This is where the file could become a real field investigation.

### 4.7 — The nature of the SEM mechanism: the power of the international investigation
- Force the federal government to come out of its silence by submitting a written, official and public
-   response on the application of its laws.
- Present the facts on a North American scale before the Council of Ministers of the Environment of
-   Canada, the United States and Mexico.
- Commission an independent investigation (the factual record) involving scientific experts,
-   interviews with authorities and unassailable technical verifications.
- Create an official evidentiary basis that can be used by parliamentarians, researchers, the media
-   and the courts.
- The Secretariat is not a civil or criminal court issuing fines or direct construction injunctions
-   against private companies.
- Its authority is exercised at the higher level of interstate accountability and transparency of
-   environmental public action.

The CCE Secretariat is not a court of first instance, but a higher international institution of
transparency and control of law enforcement. The major strength of the SEM process lies in its
unique capacity to establish an incontestable public documentary truth, instructed by independent
experts, forcing the State to answer for its actions before its trinational partners of the ACEUM
and international public opinion.

### Species at risk
The articles, in plain English:
- Art. 32 — prohibits killing, injuring, harassing, capturing or taking an individual of a species
-   listed as extinct, endangered or threatened.
- Art. 33 — prohibits damaging or destroying the residence of these individuals (their nest, their
-   burrow, their hibernacula).
- Art. 58 — prohibits destruction of critical habitat. Crucial point: this ban applies to aquatic
-   species and migratory birds even outside federal lands. It is the gateway to private land.
- Art. 73 — this is the article of the permit. The Minister may authorize an activity affecting a
-   listed species, but only if all other options have been considered, if all minimization measures
-   are taken, and if the survival of the species is not jeopardized. The decision must be reasoned
-   and entered in the public register.
- Art. 79 — requires anyone assessing the environmental effects of a project to notify the Minister if
-   a listed species is likely to be affected.

The reproach: deforestation and backfilling work takes place where species protected by federal law
live, without any permit, any verification of applicability, or any reasoned decision appearing in

### Migratory birds
- Art. 5.1(1) of the Act — prohibits the discharge of a substance harmful to migratory birds into the
-   waters or regions they frequent, or into any other place from which the substance could reach
-   them. This last clarification is decisive: it is not necessary to discharge directly into the
-   pond; the product just needs to be able to get there.
- Art. 5(1) of the Regulations — prohibits, unless permitted: (a) capturing, killing, injuring or
-   harassing a migratory bird; b) destroy or disturb an egg; c) damage, destroy, remove or disturb a
-   nest.

The heaviest word in this regulation is “disturb”. It is not necessary to kill a bird or destroy a
nest: disturbance is enough. It is on this word that the argument for work carried out during the
nesting period is based.

What is being said:
- 132 species of birds recorded during the breeding season in the plots of the Atlas of Breeding Birds
-   of Quebec corresponding to the sector; according to the 2025 provincial report, 66% were breeding
-   and 34% probable or possible breeding.
- During specific surveys in June 2016: 172 individuals of 51 species of protected migratory birds
-   counted in the planned cutting area — including the Red-winged Blackbird, the Common Yellowthroat,
-   the Fox Thrush, the Oven-crowned Warbler, the White-throated Sparrow.
- On May 22, 2026, during the critical period of protected nesting, citizens photographed the arrival
-   of a semi-trailer delivering several industrial lighting towers to the construction site. With
-   strict factual rigor, the file distinguishes the material proof of the deployment of nighttime
-   equipment from their continued use, while demonstrating the direct risk of light disturbance
-   within the meaning of Article 5(1) of the Regulation.
- Mortality of common loons, protected migratory birds, reported by Radio-Canada Enquête on October 9,
-   2025.
- Documented releases of cadmium, chromium and suspended solids into a network connected to the stream
-   — precisely the scenario targeted by section 5.1(1).
- No ECCC permits identified in public records.

An element that the Secretariat itself noted: a document from the Quebec Ministry of the
Environment, the 2024 “request for commitments”, requires Stablex to take nesting periods into
account, explicitly refers to ECCC tools to avoid harming migratory birds, and asks it to “present
measures to be put in place to protect the nests” of the pileated woodpecker, the great blue heron
and the green heron before the end of the period waiting period provided for in federal regulations.
In other words: Quebec referred to the federal framework. Which makes the absence of any trace of
federal intervention all the more striking.

### The Fisheries Act
This is the heart of the case, and by far the most powerful legal weapon. For what ? Because these
prohibitions apply regardless of who owns the land. The Loi 93 was able to transfer ownership, it
did not change the fact that there are fish downstream.
- Art. 34.4 — prohibits the exploitation of a work, business or activity that results in the death of
-   fish (except fishing itself), unless authorized.
- Art. 35 — prohibits any activity resulting in the deterioration, destruction or disturbance of fish
-   habitat, unless authorized by the Minister. It is the most cited article in Canada on wetlands.
- Art. 36(3) — prohibits the dumping or discharge of a harmful substance into waters frequented by
-   fish, or in any other place from which it may reach them. Here again: the gap matters.
- Art. 36(4) — provides for exceptions: discharges authorized by regulation, in specific quantities
-   and conditions. Added to the analysis in the second decision.
- Art. 37 — allows the Minister to require documents from the operator, then modify, restrict or cease
-   the activity.
- Art. 38 — organizes inspections. It also creates an obligation to report: everyone must report to
-   the authorities the death of fish, the destruction of their habitat or the unauthorized discharge
-   of a harmful substance, take corrective measures and produce a report.

#### a) Are there really fish?
This is the first thing a government could challenge. The response to the file is massive.
Scientific fishing carried out in August 2016 in the site's ditches — using electricity and fishing
rods — captured 155 specimens of 9 species: mudfish (108), redfin shiner (17), fivespine stickleback
(14), pumpkinseed, white sucker, pearl mullet, yellow-bellied darter, fathead minnow.

Better: it is the study of the promoter himself which recognizes that “the fish habitat is
considered potential over the entire length of the watercourse studied”. She specifies that “all of
the streams in the study area are in fact ditches […] and the water in the ditches comes from
wetlands present on the outskirts. It flows slowly towards natural streams located outside the study
area.” Some of these ditches include mature aquatic grass beds and beaver reservoirs reaching 2
meters deep. A 2020 government inspection report confirms a network of interconnected ditches
actively draining eastward.

Logical conclusion of the file: it is not an isolated hole in the middle of a field. It is a
connected hydraulic system, which leads to Lockhead Creek, then to Fauvel Lakes. Anything that falls
in travels.

#### d) Three angles which make the strength of the file
1. “Anoxia is an effect, not a cause. »

The Ville de Blainville explained the fish mortality by a lack of oxygen (press release of April 15,
2026). The expert cited in the file turns the argument around: “Anoxia, lack of oxygen, is an
EFFECT. To kill someone from anoxia, I can strangle you, or submerge your head in a bucket of water.
Death will be anoxia. But what CAUSED the anoxia? »

And the file documents a crumbling of the municipal version: first presented as a “scientific
certainty” confirmed by a private firm, the hypothesis becomes a simple “hypothesis” at the council
meeting of July 7, before the mayor admits that the City did not have any results of its own
analysis and that competence fell to the ministry. The request for access to information of June 30,
2026 confirms this: the legal affairs department responds that “no document corresponding to what
you are looking for” has been found. The file also notes that the mayor attributed the rusty color
of the water to “probably ferrous”, without scientific qualification, and that she refused to issue
a preventive advisory against swimming for the summer of 2026.

2. The fox who guards the henhouse.

The file supports, on the basis of corroborating testimonies and public documents: the private
Stablex laboratory is accredited by the Quebec ministry, which gives it the status of an independent
laboratory; sampling at observation wells is carried out jointly by the Ministry and Stablex, the
Ministry not having independent equipment; the analyzes are carried out in the Stablex laboratory.
In other words, the regulated entity analyzes its own pollution. The file also mentions a practice
of pumping wells before re-sampling, which would have the effect of diluting the concentrations, and
divergent results for the same well depending on whether they come from the Ministry or the company.
The author draws a legal conclusion: in the absence of any independent federal inspection, Ottawa
bases its abstention on data produced by the operator itself.

He also points out a discharge pipe located at the rear of the installation, discharging effluent
into a road ditch connected to Lockheed Creek and Lake Fauvel, which would not appear in the
official sampling protocol.

3. The missing declaration.

Stablex's 2024 report to the National Pollutant Release Inventory — a mandatory federal registry —
does not report any releases to any watercourse. The company publicly claims “zero discharge into
water for 26 years”. However, the file contrasts this with the overflows of June and July 2026, the
analyzes of October-November 2025 and the spill of August 2025. A cited expert makes the tax
analogy: “if you do not declare a rejection, it can be considered fraud, in the same way as when you
declare your income to Revenue Canada”. The question asked is simple: did ECCC even verify?

### Toxic substances and the right to a healthy environment
A major legal novelty, still little known. Since the Right to a Healthy Environment Act, assented to
on June 13, 2023, CEPA requires the federal government to “protect the right of every individual in
Canada to a healthy environment […] subject to reasonable limits”, respecting the principles of
non-regression, intergenerational equity and environmental justice — which includes the prevention
of harmful effects disproportionately affecting vulnerable populations.

Since 2023, there has been a legal right to a healthy environment in Canada. And this right must be
applied with future generations in mind. An adolescent who invokes this right for himself and for
those who come after him is therefore not using a figure of speech: he is relying on a brand new
legal text, which no one has yet really tested. This is, legally, the most innovative aspect of the
case.
- Between 2019 and 2024, the site would have received 120,837 tonnes of soil from the Orrington site
-   (Maine, United States), linked to the Mallinckrodt company. 84% of this volume — 101,951 tonnes —
-   would be classified as hazardous residue due to its mercury content. Mercury is a very volatile
-   metal.
- These operations take place 370 to 400 meters from residential and commercial areas.
- The company's release inventory lists fine particles PM2.5, hexavalent chromium (recognized
-   carcinogen), mercury, arsenic, nickel, crystalline silica and ammonia. An Englobe report would
-   confirm the physical presence of hexavalent chromium and crystalline silica in the dust from the
-   site.
- The framework for implementing the right to a healthy environment, required by section 5.1(1) of
-   CEPA, was due no later than June 13, 2025.

The criticism: ECCC would not have exercised any of its powers of evaluation (art. 68), collection
of information (art. 71) or risk management (art. 90 and 91) with regard to this site.

### 5.5 — Cross-cutting arguments
Articulation with commercial (art. 24.4) and constitutional (art. 35) law. The communication
highlights the overall context of the matter: on the one hand, the competitive distortion created by
the massive importation of American waste without rigorous application of federal standards; on the
other hand, the location of the site on the traditional unceded territory of the Kanien'kehá:ka
Nation and the principles of the two-row Wampum. If the Secretariat has limited the formal scope of
the investigation to only federal “environmental laws” in the strict sense of Article 24.1 (thus
excluding the aspects relating to Chapter 31 on commerce or general constitutional law), this
contextual insight remains in the public register to document the extent of the issues raised.

The Loi 93 as a lock. This is the most political argument. When a provincial government uses its
parliament to neutralize local counter-powers and close legal recourse, the author maintains, the
application of federal laws becomes “the one and only bulwark”. Ottawa's inaction would then create
“a total legal vacuum”. It relies on precedents from CCE (British Columbia Mines, SEM-98-004;
British Columbia Salmon Farms, SEM-12-001) where the existence of provincial authorizations was not
considered sufficient to exempt the federal government from acting.

The “discretion/absence of decision” distinction. This is the finest reasoning in the file. Article
24.4(2) of ACEUM explicitly recognizes that a country retains the right to exercise discretion in
matters of investigation and allocation of resources; acting reasonably in exercising this
discretion is not a violation. The author concedes this from the outset - then turns the argument
around: we can only speak of a discretionary choice if a power has first been exercised. Here, there
would be no inspection, no refusal, no reasoned decision. So not misused discretion, but a pure and
simple absence of exercise. And he adds: Canada's response will decide itself — documented
inspections would enlighten the Secretariat, while its silence would reinforce the thesis of
sustained inaction.

The argument a fortiori on the precedents of the CCE. In the historic case SEM-97-005, Canada was
the subject of a factual case for failure to apply the Fisheries Act (discharge of acids into the
St. Lawrence). Environment Canada then formally documented its refusal to intervene. William Guindon
pertinently demonstrates that the situation in Blainville reveals an even more obvious deficiency:
here, the federal apparatus has not even rendered a reasoned decision or recorded a formal
evaluation, characterizing a total and persistent omission to apply the law.

## Part 10 The Questions Everyone Asks

Can a minor really file an international complaint? Yes. The treaty speaks of “any person of a
Party”: no age requirement. A tasty paradox: he cannot go before a court in his own country without
legal representation, but he can refer the matter to a trinational commission. Moreover, it is
precisely this internal impossibility which allowed it to satisfy the criterion of private remedies.

Why target Ottawa and not Quebec, when it is Quebec that authorized everything? Two reasons. First,
the legal: the SEM mechanism is only aimed at central governments — Quebec literally cannot be
targeted. Then, the strategic: the Loi 93 closed the provincial appeals, and two courts confirmed it
in April 2025. Only the federal one remained.

How can a federal law apply to private land in Quebec? By fish and birds. Fish and their habitat, as
well as migratory birds, fall under federal jurisdiction across the country, regardless of who owns
the land. Section 36(3) of the Fisheries Act prohibits the discharge of a deleterious substance into
waters frequented by fish “or to any other place” from which it may arrive — a ditch, for example.
This is what makes this article so powerful.

If the factual record is published, what is its real impact? Its institutional, scientific and
political impact is major. The factual record is an independent international investigation led by
the three governments of North America. It allows neutral experts to be commissioned, the law
enforcement practices of federal departments to be examined, and the public factual and scientific
truth to be set in stone. In several historical precedents (such as the SEM-97-005 or North Atlantic
Right Whale affair), these reports have forced administrative reforms, fueled parliamentary work and
served as key evidence in the public space.

Has this mechanism already worked? He produced published factual files, including against Canada.
The most famous in this context is SEM-97-005, on acid discharges in the St. Lawrence: the
investigators appointed by the Secretariat concluded that Environment Canada had modified an
inspection report to the point that the signing official declared that he no longer recognized it.
The Secretariat has also just published, in December 2025, a factual file on the North Atlantic
right whale (SEM-21-003), and in August 2025 another on the Gulf of California porpoise.

How long can the procedure last given the progress of the work? Submitted in May 2026, the process
is moving through its stages at a sustained pace: following the submission of Canada's response
(72-RSP) on October 16, 2026 and the citizen's reply of October 17, 2026, the Secretariat has 60
working days (art. 24.28(1) ACEUM) to recommend a factual file, while federal ministers are legally
required to respond to BVG Petition No. 0546 by November 10, 2026. If the construction machinery
operates in the short term, the SEM mechanism establishes federal responsibility over time: each
destruction of habitat or release of contaminants occurring during Ottawa's inaction increases the
factual toll for which the government will have to answer to its ACEUM partners.

What is the value and scope of the file filed by William Guindon? The legal result is exceptional:
while the vast majority of citizen communications are rejected at the preliminary stage of
admissibility by the Secretariat of the CCE, that of William Guindon successfully passed all the
admissibility criteria of articles 24.27(1), (2) and (3) of the ACEUM. The Secretariat has validated
the probative force of its corpus of 222 documentary pieces (university hydrogeological studies,
wildlife characterizations, accredited laboratory analyzes of cadmium exceedances, ministerial
inspection records). It is a tour de force all the more remarkable because it was accomplished in a
completely autonomous and voluntary manner by a 15-year-old citizen, reaching where no municipality
or any Quebec legal action had succeeded in forcing the State to be publicly accountable.

## Part 11 Complete Glossary

- ACEUM

Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement, in force since July 1, 2020. Replaces NAFTA. Its chapter 24
focuses on the environment. Called USMCA in English, T-MEC in Spanish.
- ACE

Environmental cooperation agreement, signed in parallel with the ACEUM. It is he who organizes the
CCE itself.
- Anoxia

Absence of oxygen in the water. Common cause of fish mortality — but, as the case points out, it is
the mechanism of death, not its origin.
- BAPE

Bureau d'audiences publiques sur l'environnement (Québec Bureau of Public Environmental Hearings),
Quebec organization which holds hearings and provides opinions to the government. Its report No. 371
focused on this project and recommended against authorization.
- Gag

Exceptional parliamentary procedure which limits the time of debate to force the rapid adoption of a
bill. Used for Loi 93 March 27-28, 2025.
- CCE

Commission de coopération environnementale. Trinational organization created in 1994, based in
Montreal. Three bodies: Council, Secretariat, Joint Public Advisory Committee.
- Cell (burial)

Waterproof compartment dug into the ground, isolated by a membrane, where waste is placed. Stablex
operates five; the sixth is the subject of the dispute.
- CER (rare endpoint)

Reference threshold for sediments: level below which virtually no toxic effects on bottom-dwelling
organisms are expected.
- CVAC (chronic aquatic life criterion)

Reference threshold for water: concentration beyond which prolonged exposure harms aquatic
organisms.
- Hexavalent chromium

Particularly toxic form of chromium, recognized carcinogen by inhalation. Not to be confused with
trivalent chromium, which is much less dangerous.
- Communication / Observation

The two names for the same thing: the citizen complaint. The treaty says “observations”, the
Commission says “communications”.
- Advice (from CCE)

The environment ministers of the three countries, or their representatives. This is the body that
votes on the creation of a factual file — by 2 votes out of 3.
- Decree 515-2025

Quebec decree authorizing the project and setting its conditions. The provincial notices of
non-compliance mentioned in the file relate to compliance with these conditions.
- Determination

The written and reasoned decision of the Secretariat at each stage of filtering. This is not a
judgment.
- Factual record

The possible end product of the process: a public investigation report bringing together technical,
scientific and legal data, interviews with officials and analyzes from independent experts. He
states the facts without concluding, for the public to judge.
- ECCC

Environment and Climate Change Canada. Its Enforcement Branch is the agency responsible for
enforcing federal environmental laws.
- Take legal action

Legal expression meaning “to be able to act before a court”. A minor cannot go to court alone.
- Critical habitat

SARA concept: habitat necessary for the survival or recovery of a listed species. Its destruction is
prohibited by article 58.
- Hibernacle

Underground shelter where reptiles spend the winter. Central argument of the case: the snakes are
inaccessible there before May, therefore invisible during deforestation ending on April 15.
- ICP-MS

Inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry: reference laboratory technique for measuring metals at
very low concentrations. This is the method used by the WaterShed campaign.
- NPRI

National inventory of pollutant releases: federal register where companies are required to declare
their releases. An inaccurate statement is subject to prosecution under CEPA.
- LCOM / RCOM

Migratory Birds Convention Act, 1994 and Regulations (2022 version). Sometimes abbreviated ROM in
the complaint.
- CEPA

Canadian Environmental Protection Act (1999), modernized in 2023 to include the right to a healthy
environment.
- THE P

Endangered Species Act (2002). Its appendix 1 lists protected species, classified from “concerning”
to “extinct”.
- Loi 93

Quebec law (L.Q. 2025, c. 7), official title: Act concerning in particular the transfer of ownership
of an immovable in the Ville de Blainville. Adopted under gag order on March 28, 2025 by 61 votes to
31. It expropriates the City for $17 million and authorizes “no appeal for judicial review […]
except on a question of jurisdiction”.
- MELCCFP (Québec Ministry of the Environment, the Fight against Climate Change, Wildlife and Parks)

Ministère de l'Environnement, de la Lutte contre les changements climatiques, de la Faune et des
Parcs (Québec Ministry of the Environment, the Fight against Climate Change, Wildlife and Parks)
from Quebec.
- MY

Suspended solids: solid particles floating in water. They make the water cloudy, clog the gills and
smother the spawning grounds.
- DFO

Fisheries and Oceans Canada, responsible for enforcing the Fisheries Act.
- “Failing to apply effectively”

The key formula of the entire chapter 24. It does not aim at the quality of the laws, but at
enforcing them in practice: inspect, investigate, authorize, sanction.
- Environmental petition

Canadian federal mechanism administered by the Office of the Auditor General: anyone residing in
Canada can raise environmental concerns with federal ministers, who are required to respond. It is
this mechanism which unlocked the criterion of private remedies.
- Prima facie

Latin expression: “at first sight”. Prima facie evidence is sufficient to warrant review, not to
establish guilt.
- S.E.M.

English acronym for Submissions on Enforcement Matters. Refers to the entire citizen complaint
process of articles 24.27 and 24.28.
- Sphagnum

Moss characteristic of peat bogs, which retains water like a sponge and acidifies the environment.
Its mounds provide shelter for the four-toed salamander.
- Bog

Humid environment where organic matter accumulates without completely decomposing due to lack of
oxygen. Filters water, stores huge amounts of carbon, is home to specialized wildlife. Once
destroyed, it cannot be reconstituted on the scale of a human life.
- Turbidity

Measurement of water turbidity. High turbidity after rain, downstream of a construction site,
indicates sediment leaching.
- Two-row wampum

Ancestral treaty between the Haudenosaunee nations and Europeans, materialized by a belt of pearls
with two parallel rows: two boats navigating on the same river without one directing the other.
Invoked in the complaint, but excluded from the legal analysis.

### 12.2 — Methodical instruction and categorization by the Secretariat
Faced with the exceptional richness of the evidentiary corpus submitted by William Guindon - more
than 200 scientific, technical, cartographic and legal documents - the Secretariat of the CCE
proceeded to a methodical structuring of the file into ten major categories of evidentiary analysis:
- Impact studies and ecological characterizations: Integration of Englobe reports (2020 and 2023) and
-   Quebec government inventories documenting protected species and their habitats.
- Independent hydrochemical campaigns: Analysis of WaterShed Monitoring data highlighting excesses in
-   heavy metals (notably cadmium, arsenic, lead).
- Ministerial inspections and compliance: Taking into account provincial inspection reports noting
-   encroachment on the riparian strip and vegetation management.
- Failure to issue federal authorizations: Exploitation of requests for access to information
-   demonstrating the inaction of the Canadian federal apparatus.
- Field verifications and visual documentation: Photographic records documenting episodes of aquatic
-   mortality and sediment transfers during rainy weather.

This rigorous educational approach made it possible to validate the admissibility of the
communication on all of the fundamental laws, establishing a historical precedent for a citizen
initiative led by a 15-year-old author.
