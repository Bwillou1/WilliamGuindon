## 1. Sampling results from December 2023 (Cadmium at 320×)

Cadmium (Cd) is one of the most toxic heavy metals for living organisms. Proven carcinogen,
bioaccumulative and persistent in perpetuity in the environment, it is subject to the most rigorous
environmental standards in Canada and Quebec. The criterion for the protection of aquatic life
(chronic effect in fresh water) established by the Ministry of the Environment of Quebec (MELCCFP)
is set at 0.00025 mg/L (i.e. 0.25 µg/L).

During the independent sampling campaign conducted in December 2023, samples were collected using a
rigorous upstream-downstream observation grid. While the stations located upstream of the forest
presented concentrations below the analytical detection limit (< 0.0001 mg/L), the stations located
in the ditches receiving runoff and resurgence water downstream from cells 1 to 4 displayed alarming
values:

## 2. The WaterShed Monitoring hydrogeological study (June 2026)

One of the traditional arguments put forward to justify the burial of hazardous waste in this sector
is based on the presumed presence of an impermeable marine clay base from the Champlain Sea.
However, the in-depth field study carried out by WaterShed Monitoring in June 2026 invalidated this
geological simplification:
- Heterogeneity of loose deposits: The thickness of the clay layer is highly variable and in places
-   presents discontinuities (hydrogeological windows) occupied by conductive silty sands.
- Hydraulic gradient towards the southwest: The underground hydraulic load naturally pushes pore water
-   from the peaty plateau towards the surrounding depressions, with an actual percolation speed
-   higher than the developer's theoretical modeling.
- Physico-chemical interaction with peat bog water: Peat bog water, naturally acidic and rich in humic
-   and fulvic acids, accelerates the dissolution of heavy metals trapped in alkaline residues when
-   they come into contact with environmental seeps.

## 3. The ecological vulnerability of Lacs Fauvel

Former sand and gravel pits restored by the community, the Fauvel lakes have been transformed over
the decades into an exceptional ecological refuge in the heart of Blainville. This area is home to a
rich community of fish (bass, yellow perch, pike), turtles and more than 150 recorded species of
birds.

Hydrological data demonstrate that the peripheral drainage network of the Grande Tourbière (Great
Peat Bog) flows by gravity towards the tributaries which feed the Fauvel lakes basin before joining
the Mille Îles River, the source of drinking water for more than 400,000 residents of the northern
suburbs of Montreal. Any uncontrolled toxic release at Stablex level indirectly contaminates this
irreplaceable collective heritage.

## 4. The official position of Stablex Canada Inc. and second opinion

For the sake of objectivity and journalistic rigor, it is appropriate to set out in detail the
arguments formulated by Stablex and its consultant experts in their documents filed with the
Ministry of the Environment (MELCCFP):
- The concept of stabilized waste: Stablex maintains that heavy metals are integrated into the
-   crystalline network of calcium hydrosilicates during the cement reaction, making them insoluble
-   even in the presence of infiltration water.
- Official piezometer monitoring: The company maintains a network of around thirty regulatory
-   piezometers inspected periodically and maintains that the data transmitted to the ministry
-   respects the thresholds prescribed by its operating authorizations.
- Hypothesis of historical history: The northern sector of Blainville hosted the Bouchard Plan weapons
-   installations during the Second World War. Stablex raises the possibility that old embankments
-   unrelated to its activities explain certain specific anomalies in the surface ditches.

The independent experts, however, retort that the chemical signature of the samples (cadmium/zinc
ratio and presence of concentrated sulfates characteristic of the Stablex neutralization process)
does not correspond in any way to the military explosives of the Bouchard Plan, but rather to the
hydrometallurgy residues treated in the factory.

## 5. Legal implications: Fisheries Act and remedies CCE

In Canadian federal law, section 36(3) of the Fisheries Act establishes an absolute and strict
prohibition: it is prohibited to deposit or authorize the deposit of a harmful substance in waters
where fish live, or in a place where it risks entering such waters.

By demonstrating the existence of hydrographic connections between the ditches of the peat bog and
the fish habitats of Lacs Fauvel and the Rivière des Mille Îles, the contamination data places the
Government of Canada before its international responsibilities of monitoring and sanctions, which
are the subject of the formal procedure underway before the Secretariat of the CCE (Commission for
Environmental Cooperation).
