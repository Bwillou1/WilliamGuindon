rights of future generations. At the age of 15, he brought the Grande Tourbière de Blainville (Great
Blainville Peat Bog) file before the Commission de coopération environnementale (ACEUM) and the

### Recognition of unceded traditional territory
I recognize with deep respect that the Grande Tourbière de Blainville and the lands of the Lower
Laurentians on which my alert and research efforts are deployed are an integral part of the unceded
traditional territory of the Kanien'kehá:ka (Mohawk) nation, millennial guardian of the lands and
waters of Tiohtià:ke and the Mille-Îles River basin. This natural space also constitutes a historic
place of exchanges and shared passage with the Anishinaabeg nations and the entire Haudenosaunee
confederacy. I salute their continued stewardship of Mother Earth.
- Kanien'kehá:ka: “People of the Flint” (Mohawk Nation), guardians of the Eastern Gate of the
-   Haudenosaunee Confederacy.
- Tiohtià:ke: “Where the currents meet” (traditional Mohawk name for the archipelago and surrounding
-   river basins).
- Haudenosaunee: “The People of the Longhouse” (Six Nations Confederacy).

## The Stablex file

### A thousand-year-old ecosystem facing the landfill of hazardous waste
The Grande Tourbière de Blainville is a thousand-year-old ecosystem located in Blainville, included
by the Quebec Ministry of the Environment in the Atlas of Territories of Interest for Conservation
in the St. Lawrence Lowlands. The Stablex site, in operation since 1983, is the only center
authorized in Quebec for the final burial of materials resulting from the treatment by stabilization
and solidification of inorganic industrial residues.
- September 2023: The Bureau d'audiences publiques sur l'environnement (BAPE) concludes that the
-   expansion project (Cell 6) is “premature”.
- December 2023: Citizen analyzes reveal cadmium concentrations up to 320 times higher than
-   environmental standards in nearby surface waters.
- March 2025: The Quebec government adopts Projet de loi 93 under gag order, forcing the transfer of
-   land belonging to Ville de Blainville (City of Blainville), including 278,000 m² of wetlands, to
-   allow the expansion of the site.
- Blocked appeals: The law contains clauses excluding any legal appeal on the merits of the decision,
-   which the Centre québécois du droit de l'environnement (CQDE) describes as a serious violation of
-   the right to an effective remedy.
- Imports: Approximately 41% of the materials buried in Blainville come from outside Quebec, including
-   approximately 29% from the United States, which establishes the cross-border dimension of the
-   appeal filed with the CCE.

It is in this context that William Guindon chooses the path of law: rather than confrontation, he
mobilizes national and international mechanisms: BAPE, ACEUM, UN, to demand that Canada and Quebec
respect their own environmental obligations.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Understand everything about citizen initiatives and the SEM-26-003 file

### Who is William Guindon?
William Guindon (born in 2011 in Quebec) is a Quebec environmental activist and student in the
Laurentians. Author of the communication SEM-26-003 filed at the age of 14, he became at 15 the
first minor in the history of Environmental Submissions (SEM) to obtain a positive determination of
admissibility before the Commission de coopération environnementale (CCE / ACEUM). Consult his
detailed biography, the Stablex chronology or the press sheet.

### What is the SEM-26-003 file with CCE?
Communication SEM-26-003 (“Landfilling of hazardous materials in Blainville”) denounces the failure
to apply Canadian federal environmental laws (migratory birds, species at risk, cadmium discharge)
in the context of the expansion of the Stablex site into 278,000 m² of wetlands. Find the complete
chronology of the file.

### Why is Grande Tourbière de Blainville threatened?
The Grande Tourbière de Blainville is home to exceptional biodiversity and constitutes a major
carbon sink. The expansion project would allow millions of tons of toxic waste to be buried,
destroying 278,000 m² of rare ecosystems and contaminating waters with cadmium measured at more than
320 times the standards. View the perimeters on the interactive GIS map.

### What are the next steps after October 16, 2026?
On October 16, 2026, the Government of Canada is due to submit its official response. The CCE
Secretariat will then determine under Article 24.28 of ACEUM whether it recommends the development
of an independent Factual Record for vote by the Trinational Council of Ministers. Follow the
official count live.
