Indigenous Nations Space

Recognition of unceded traditional territory & ancestral rights

The Great Peatland of Blainville is located on the unceded traditional territory of the Kanien'kehá:ka (Mohawk) Nation, ancestral guardians of these lands and waters. Protecting this ecosystem aligns with the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP) and the fundamental right of future generations to a healthy environment (UNGA Resolution 76/300).

Land Stewardship & Intergenerational Environmental Justice

"We do not inherit the Earth from our ancestors, we borrow it from our children." File SEM-26-003 rests on the shared responsibility to preserve rare wetlands against toxic industrial waste disposal.

Introduction

An early commitment, an uncompromising determination

William Guindon (born August 3, 2011 in Sainte-Marthe-sur-le-Lac, Quebec) is a Quebec environmental activist recognized for his commitment to protecting ecosystems and the rights of future generations. Author at 14 of an official submission filed with the Commission for Environmental Cooperation (CEC) under CUSMA and of a formal UN deposition, he secured at 15 a historic positive determination from the CEC regarding the Great Peatland of Blainville.

Raised in the Laurentides region, he attends Externat Sacré-Cœur, a private secondary school in Rosemère, where he received the Méritas Award for intellectual curiosity (2023) and for commitment and humanism (2024). A member of the Royal Canadian Army Cadets, he reached the Silver Star level. From age ten, he volunteered with seniors in long-term care homes and coordinated his school's community garden in partnership with the Town of Rosemère.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Understanding the citizen action and CEC dossier SEM-26-003

Who is William Guindon?

William Guindon (born August 3, 2011 in Sainte-Marthe-sur-le-Lac, Quebec) is a Canadian environmental activist and student at Externat Sacré-Cœur in Rosemère. Author of submission SEM-26-003 at 14, he became at 15 the first minor in the history of Citizen Submissions on Enforcement Matters (SEM) to secure a positive determination before the Commission for Environmental Cooperation (CEC / CUSMA). Read his biography, Stablex timeline, or press kit.

What is submission SEM-26-003 before the CEC?

Submission SEM-26-003 ("Hazardous Waste Disposal in Blainville") challenges Canada's failure to enforce federal environmental laws (Migratory Birds Convention Act, Species at Risk Act, Fisheries Act) regarding the expansion of the Stablex toxic landfill into 278,000 m² of peatland wetlands. See the full timeline of the case.

Why is the Great Peatland of Blainville threatened by Stablex?

The Great Peatland of Blainville is a critical carbon sink and biodiversity sanctuary. The Cell 6 project permits the disposal of millions of tonnes of hazardous waste, destroying 278,000 m² of rare wetlands and creating heavy metal leaching risks (cadmium measured up to 320x norms). Explore the interactive GIS map to view the impacted perimeters.

What are the next milestones after October 16, 2026?

On October 16, 2026, Canada must submit its formal response. The CEC Secretariat will review the response under Article 24.28 of CUSMA to decide whether to recommend developing an independent Factual Record for a vote by the Trinational Council of Ministers.
