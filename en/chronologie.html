Timeline of the SEM-26-003 International Citizen Recourse

Comprehensive timeline tracing every major milestone: from the formal BAPE rejection (Report 371) and forced municipal expropriation under Bill 93 to the 10 procedural steps of the Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement (USMCA) and projected recourse horizons through 2040.

1. Quebec Legislative & Environmental Background: BAPE 371 and Bill 93

The tipping point: The unequivocal adverse finding by the BAPE Commission of Inquiry in September 2023, followed by the fast-tracked passage of Bill 93 under closure on March 28, 2025, to forcibly expropriate the City of Blainville for the exclusive benefit of Stablex.

The timeline originates from the rigorous scientific rejection of the Cell 6 expansion (278,000 m² of ombrotrophic peatland). BAPE Report 371 documented a decisive factual point: existing Cell 5 possesses sufficient residual disposal capacity until 2030, rendering the premature destruction of the wetland wholly unwarranted.

Faced with the City of Blainville's refusal to cede municipal conservation lands, the Quebec government enacted Bill 93 under gag order, stripping municipal jurisdiction and transferring the land to Stablex, triggering ongoing constitutional litigation before the Quebec Superior Court.

2. The 4 Completed CEC Steps (May to October 2026)

Historic procedural milestone: Citizen submission SEM-26-003 was determined admissible by the international Secretariat of the CEC on August 17, 2026, legally obliging the Government of Canada to submit its official response.
  • Step 1 · Preliminary Filing & Acknowledgment (May 4 – June 3, 2026): Initial procedural exchanges and verification under Article 24.27(1).
  • Step 2 · Formal Submission of 200+ Pages & 222 Exhibits (July 16, 2026): William Guindon, aged 15, files the comprehensive citizen submission alleging non-enforcement of the Fisheries Act (cadmium discharges exceeding aquatic life benchmarks by up to 320 times) and the Species at Risk Act.
  • Step 3 · Positive Determination DET-26-003 (August 17, 2026): Groundbreaking determination finding that the submission meets all strict criteria of Articles 24.27(2) and (3), setting Canada's deadline for October 16, 2026.
  • Step 4 · Canada's Response (72-RSP) & Submitter's Legal Rebuttal (Oct. 16–17, 2026): Canada requests dismissal citing the submitter's own petition to the Auditor General. On October 17, William Guindon files observations establishing that an Auditor General petition is not an ongoing judicial or quasi-judicial proceeding under Article 24.27(4)(a).

3. Step 5 Underway: Secretariat Review & Recommendation

Under Article 24.28(1) of the USMCA, the CEC Secretariat has 60 working days to evaluate Canada's response and recommend whether file SEM-26-003 warrants the development of an independent international Factual Record to the CEC Council (environment ministers of Canada, the United States, and Mexico).

4. Projected Steps 6 to 10 and Follow-up Horizon Through 2040

Why this recourse provides enduring hope: Existing Cell 5 capacity is sufficient through 2030 (BAPE), while a complete CEC factual record procedure requires 3.3 to 4.1 years on average. This timeline overlap creates a vital window of strategic protection.
  • Step 6 · Council Vote (two-thirds majority): Historical CEC average of 796 calendar days (range of 617 to 957 days).
  • Step 7 · Draft Factual Record Preparation: Expert factual investigation, fieldwork, and draft preparation (average 129 calendar days).
  • Steps 8–10 · Party Comments, Final Vote & Public Release: Independent factual findings made publicly accessible across all three nations.
  • Horizon 2027–2040 · Continuous Oversight & Compensatory Litigation: Ongoing environmental compliance, NGO litigation around 2030, and remediation oversight through May 2040 (+14 year horizon).
