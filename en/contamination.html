Expertise Scientifique & Hydrogéologie

Cadmium Contamination Analyses and Hydrogeological Vulnerability in Blainville

Scientific investigative report on heavy metal concentrations measured downstream of the Stablex landfill site: December 2023 sampling campaigns, June 2026 WaterShed Monitoring study, threats to the Fauvel Lakes, and adversarial comparison with industry claims.

← Back to main file: Page pilier Stablex Blainville ↗

1. Sampling results from December 2023 (Cadmium at 320×)

Résultats d'échantillonnage : In December 2023, surface water sampling conducted downstream of Stablex's historical cells in Blainville revealed a cadmium concentration of 0.080 mg/L. This level exceeds the freshwater aquatic life protection criterion (0.00025 mg/L) by 320 times, signaling acute contamination of the drainage ditches.

Cadmium (Cd) is one of the most toxic heavy metals for living organisms. Proven carcinogen, bioaccumulative and persistent in perpetuity in the environment, it is subject to the most rigorous environmental standards in Canada and Quebec. The criterion for the protection of aquatic life (chronic effect in fresh water) established by the Ministry of the Environment of Quebec (MELCCFP) is set at 0.00025 mg/L (i.e. 0.25 µg/L).

During the independent sampling campaign conducted in December 2023, samples were collected using a rigorous upstream-downstream observation grid. While the stations located upstream of the forest presented concentrations below the analytical detection limit (< 0.0001 mg/L), the stations located in the ditches receiving runoff and resurgence water downstream from cells 1 to 4 displayed alarming values:

2. The WaterShed Monitoring hydrogeological study (June 2026)

Expertise WaterShed Monitoring : Conducted in June 2026 by independent specialists, the WaterShed Monitoring hydrogeological study confirmed piezometric continuity between the Stablex landfill zone and surrounding watersheds. It demonstrates that leachate migrates along permeable sandy pathways toward receiving wetlands.

One of the traditional arguments put forward to justify the burial of hazardous waste in this sector is based on the presumed presence of an impermeable marine clay base from the Champlain Sea. However, the in-depth field study carried out by WaterShed Monitoring in June 2026 invalidated this geological simplification:

  • Heterogeneity of loose deposits: The thickness of the clay layer is highly variable and in places presents discontinuities (hydrogeological windows) occupied by conductive silty sands.
  • Hydraulic gradient towards the southwest: The underground hydraulic load naturally pushes pore water from the peaty plateau towards the surrounding depressions, with an actual percolation speed higher than the developer's theoretical modeling.
  • Physico-chemical interaction with peat bog water: Peat bog water, naturally acidic and rich in humic and fulvic acids, accelerates the dissolution of heavy metals trapped in alkaline residues when they come into contact with environmental seeps.

3. The ecological vulnerability of Lacs Fauvel

Impact on the Fauvel Lakes: Located less than 2 kilometers directly downstream hydrogeologically from the Stablex site, the Fauvel Lakes form a heavily visited municipal nature park. Continuous heavy metal infiltration poses a direct threat to fish populations, migratory birds, and park users.

Former sand and gravel pits restored by the community, the Fauvel lakes have been transformed over the decades into an exceptional ecological refuge in the heart of Blainville. This area is home to a rich community of fish (bass, yellow perch, pike), turtles and more than 150 recorded species of birds.

Hydrological data demonstrate that the peripheral drainage network of the Grande Tourbière (Great Peat Bog) flows by gravity towards the tributaries which feed the Fauvel lakes basin before joining the Mille Îles River, the source of drinking water for more than 400,000 residents of the northern suburbs of Montreal. Any uncontrolled toxic release at Stablex level indirectly contaminates this irreplaceable collective heritage.

4. The official position of Stablex Canada Inc. and second opinion

Position de l'industrie : Stablex Canada Inc. asserts that its fixation-solidification process is permanent and that its facilities have generated no groundwater contamination. The company maintains that observed metal traces result either from regional geological background levels or from separate historical industrial activities.

For the sake of objectivity and journalistic rigor, it is appropriate to set out in detail the arguments formulated by Stablex and its consultant experts in their documents filed with the Ministry of the Environment (MELCCFP):

  • The concept of stabilized waste: Stablex maintains that heavy metals are integrated into the crystalline network of calcium hydrosilicates during the cement reaction, making them insoluble even in the presence of infiltration water.
  • Official piezometer monitoring: The company maintains a network of around thirty regulatory piezometers inspected periodically and maintains that the data transmitted to the ministry respects the thresholds prescribed by its operating authorizations.
  • Hypothesis of historical history: The northern sector of Blainville hosted the Bouchard Plan weapons installations during the Second World War. Stablex raises the possibility that old embankments unrelated to its activities explain certain specific anomalies in the surface ditches.

The independent experts, however, retort that the chemical signature of the samples (cadmium/zinc ratio and presence of concentrated sulfates characteristic of the Stablex neutralization process) does not correspond in any way to the military explosives of the Bouchard Plan, but rather to the hydrometallurgy residues treated in the factory.

5. Legal implications: Fisheries Act and remedies CCE

Federal Legal Scope: The discharge or runoff of cadmium at toxic concentrations into waters tributary to fish habitat directly contravenes section 36(3) of the Fisheries Act of Canada. This element forms a central evidentiary pillar of international submission SEM-26-003 filed with the CEC.

In Canadian federal law, section 36(3) of the Fisheries Act establishes an absolute and strict prohibition: it is prohibited to deposit or authorize the deposit of a harmful substance in waters where fish live, or in a place where it risks entering such waters.

By demonstrating the existence of hydrographic connections between the ditches of the peat bog and the fish habitats of Lacs Fauvel and the Rivière des Mille Îles, the contamination data places the Government of Canada before its international responsibilities of monitoring and sanctions, which are the subject of the formal procedure underway before the Secretariat of the CCE (Commission for Environmental Cooperation).
