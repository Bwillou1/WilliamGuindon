Part 01 The story, told simply

In Blainville, in the Laurentians north of Montreal, there is a large swamp called Grande Tourbière (Great Peat Bog). A bog is a spongy wetland where dead vegetation accumulates without completely rotting. These environments filter water, store carbon and shelter species that are found nowhere else.

Next door is a company called Stablex. Its job: to receive dangerous industrial waste – sludge filled with heavy metals, for example – to mix it with cement to “freeze” it, then to bury it in large airtight pits called cells. Its first five cells reach saturation. He needs a sixth.

The land coveted for this cell no. 6 belonged to the Ville de Blainville (City of Blainville), which refused to cede it. In March 2025, the Quebec government resolved the problem in an expeditious manner: it passed a special law, Loi 93 (Bill 93), passed overnight under a gag order (an emergency procedure that cuts short the debate). This law expropriates the City, pays it $17 million, transfers the land to Stablex — and in the process forecloses most recourses before the courts. The City and the Metropolitan Community of Montreal tried to challenge it: they lost in the Superior Court on April 2, 2025, then in the Court of Appeal on April 16, 2025.

The machines have arrived. The trees have fallen. It was at this moment that William Guindon, a citizen of Blainville then aged 14 at the start of his efforts (now 15 years old), opened an unprecedented legal front: rather than attacking Quebec or Stablex – two avenues blocked by the Loi 93 – he questioned the direct responsibility of Ottawa.

His legal reasoning is implacable :

Quebec can legislate on the transfer of land, but there are Canadian federal laws that protect fish habitat, migratory birds and species at risk across the country, including on private or municipal land. These laws fall under the exclusive jurisdiction of Ottawa. And Ottawa remained completely silent.

The strength of the case lies in this demonstration: it does not criticize the federal government for having poorly exercised its discretionary power, but for never having exercised it. No known federal inspections, no permits issued or refused under the Fisheries Act or the Species at Risk Act, no reasoned decisions.

By mobilizing the international mechanism of chapter 24 of the ACEUM (SEM process) from May 1, 2026, then by filing on July 16, 2026 a consolidated communication of exceptional density - 15-page brief, 102-page analytical annex and 222 evidentiary documents -, he has built a file that stands the test of international filtering.

Commission de coopération environnementale, created in 1994, is headquartered at 1001 Robert-Bourassa Boulevard in Montreal. It has three bodies : the Council (the Ministers of the Environment of the three countries - it is he who votes), the Secretariat (the permanent administration, which investigates complaints) and the Joint Public Consultative Committee (of citizens of the three countries, consultative role).

Part 03 The place: three superimposed layers

3.1 — The Grande Tourbière de Blainville

A wetland complex which, according to provincial inspection reports on file, includes “a marsh, a swamp, shallow water and a peat bog”. There we find plants indicative of wetlands — pitcher plant (a carnivorous plant), cotton grass — growing on permanent sphagnum mats. The Bureau d'audiences publiques sur l'environnement (BAPE), in its report No. 371, highlighted the “exceptional ecological value” of the sector and advised the government against authorizing the project.

A geological detail that matters enormously. The area is underlain by thick deposits of clay from the Champlain Sea — a sea that covered the St. Lawrence Valley about 10,000 years ago. These clays, 15 meters thick on the surface and up to 50 meters deep, are subject to “deep ravinement” : the water creates tree-shaped networks. In practical terms, this means that any sediment or contaminant that reaches the ground is transported quickly and far to waterways. It is not a ground that holds back; it is a ground that leads.

3.2 — Cell No. 6

The land in question is lot 6 695 053 of the Quebec land register. According to media coverage and BAPE (Québec Bureau of Public Environmental Hearings), it includes approximately 9 hectares of wetlands and 58 hectares of woodlands. According to the proponent's impact study, the 52-hectare study area contains 29.2 hectares of wetlands (56% of the surface area), including a 19.1-hectare tree swamp. The planned backfilling would cover 278,000 m², or 27.8 hectares. (These different figures relate to different perimeters — see Part 9.)

The agreement in principle signed on March 11, 2020 between the City and the developer provides for 24 hours a day, 7 days a week operation. The new site would allow Stablex to operate for around forty more years, until around 2065.

La réalité physique de l'enfouissement soulevée au dossier
Si le discours promotionnel évoque des « blocs » solidifiés, l'expertise technique versée au dossier (transcription horodatée) décrit plutôt une boue cimenteuse semi-liquide sujette à une séparation de phase : « C'est comme du ciment mal fait. […] Il y a de l'eau qui flotte sur le ciment. […] Ce ne sont pas des blocs, c'est des boues qui sont enfouies. » Quant à la géomembrane étanche, le spécialiste interrogé y voit « une fausse sécurité » à long terme face à la dégradation chimique, aux mouvements de terrain ou aux séismes. Ces éléments techniques versés au registre visent à éclairer les risques hydrogéologiques à long terme pour le milieu récepteur.

3.3 — What was there before: Camp Bouchard

This is perhaps the most surprising element of the file. From 1942 to 1980, this same land was home to Camp Bouchard, a munitions production plant and explosives depot operated by the Canadian Department of National Defense. In other words: the soil was first contaminated by the federal government itself.

The site still appears on the Federal Contaminated Sites Inventory (number 06875001), maintained by the Treasury Board Secretariat. According to the data cited in the complaint:

  • Identified contaminants: metals, metalloids and organometallics in groundwater and soil
  • File status: “Active” — it is not closed
  • Management strategy: “Additional assessment” — work is not completed
  • Expenditures in 2025-2026: $0 on evaluation, $0 on restoration, $0 on monitoring
  • Area restored in 2025-2026: 0 hectare
  • Historical total expenditures: approximately $147,000 for a 4-hectare military site operated for 38 years

Stablex itself publicly attributes part of the metals detected to this previous military use. The author draws two formidable questions from this: (1) did the Ministry of Defense decontaminate before ceding the land? A freedom of information request reportedly found no documentation of decontamination work. (2) If the metals found in the stream come in part from the former federal military site, why has Ottawa never investigated?

Part 04 The mechanism, from A to Z

4.1 — Where does this tool come from

In 1994, when signing NAFTA, the three countries feared that one state would be tempted to attract factories by turning a blind eye to pollution. They therefore created, in parallel, an environmental agreement equipped with a citizen valve. When NAFTA was replaced by ACEUM on July 1, 2020, this valve was retained and written directly into the trade treaty, in articles 24.27 and 24.28.

Curiosity of vocabulary: the official French text of ACEUM speaks of “observations on questions of application”. The Commission continues to use the word “communications”. Both mean exactly the same thing: a citizen complaint. The acronym SEM comes from the English Submissions on Enforcement Matters.

4.2 — First question: what is an “environmental law”?

All the legal sorting of the decision follows from this definition. Here is the official text, in article 24.1 of ACEUM: > “environmental law means a law or regulation of a Party, or one of its provisions […] the primary > object of which is the protection of the environment, or the prevention of a danger to health or > human life, by, as the case may be: a) the prevention, reduction or control of the discharge […] of > pollutants or contaminants; (b) the control of dangerous or toxic chemicals, substances, materials > or waste […]; (c) the protection or conservation of wild flora and fauna, including endangered > species, their habitat and specially protected natural areas […]” “law or regulation means […] for > Canada, an Act of the Parliament of Canada or a regulation made under an Act of the Parliament of > Canada which may be enforced by action of the central government. »

« loi environnementale désigne une loi ou un règlement d'une Partie, ou une de ses dispositions […] dont l'objet premier est la protection de l'environnement, ou la prévention d'un danger pour la santé ou la vie humaine, par, selon le cas : a) la prévention, la réduction ou le contrôle du rejet […] de polluants ou de contaminants ; b) le contrôle des produits chimiques, substances, matières ou déchets dangereux ou toxiques […] ; c) la protection ou la conservation de la flore et de la faune sauvages, y compris des espèces menacées, de leur habitat et des zones naturelles spécialement protégées […] »

« loi ou règlement désigne […] pour le Canada, une loi du Parlement du Canada ou un règlement pris sous le régime d'une loi du Parlement du Canada pouvant être mis en application par une action du gouvernement central. »
EN CLAIR

For an article of law to be retained by the CCE (Commission for Environmental Cooperation), it must meet three strict conditions: (1) have as its primary purpose the protection of the environment or human health; (2) be a federal law or regulation passed in Ottawa; (3) fall under the application of the federal government. It is this surgical filter that allowed the Secretariat to focus the investigation on the four federal ecological pillars invoked in the communication (Fisheries Act, SARA, LCOM, CEPA), while ruling out provisions of a purely commercial or general constitutional nature.

Dans sa détermination du 17 août 2026, le Secrétariat souligne la rigueur de son examen : il ne se contente pas d'une appréciation globale de l'existence d'une « question sérieuse », mais évalue chaque critère des paragraphes 24.27(1), (2) et (3) individuellement. Un seul critère manquant suffit à entraîner le rejet d'une communication — le sort réservé à la grande majorité des plaintes déposées en Amérique du Nord. En s'appuyant sur sa jurisprudence constante (Tortue caouanne 2021, Terminal Fairview 2021, Marsouin du golfe de Californie 2021), le Secrétariat conclut que le dossier consolidé par William Guindon franchit avec succès chacun de ces filtres exigeants.

An important counterbalance, that said: the Secretariat also recalls that these criteria “must not constitute an insurmountable procedural obstacle” and must be interpreted in the broad sense. He cites three precedents in this sense (Loggerhead Turtle 2021, Fairview Terminal 2021, Gulf of California Porpoise 2021). The door is narrow, but we must not barricade it.

4.5 — Deadlines, calculated to the nearest day

The Secretariat explains in a footnote how it counts: in calendar days; the day of departure is not counted, the day of arrival is; if the deadline falls on a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday, it slides to the next working day; a document received on a non-working day is deemed received on the following working day.

Allégation 3 — l'axe principal

The Fisheries Act

Articles 34.4, 35, 36(3), 36(4), 37 et 38

This is the heart of the case, and by far the most powerful legal weapon. For what ? Because these prohibitions apply regardless of who owns the land. The Loi 93 was able to transfer ownership, it did not change the fact that there are fish downstream.

The articles, in plain English :

  • Art. 34.4 — prohibits the exploitation of a work, business or activity that results in the death of fish (except fishing itself), unless authorized.
  • Art. 35 — prohibits any activity resulting in the deterioration, destruction or disturbance of fish habitat, unless authorized by the Minister. It is the most cited article in Canada on wetlands.
  • Art. 36(3) — prohibits the dumping or discharge of a harmful substance into waters frequented by fish, or in any other place from which it may reach them. Here again : the gap matters.
  • Art. 36(4) — provides for exceptions : discharges authorized by regulation, in specific quantities and conditions. Added to the analysis in the second decision.
  • Art. 37 — allows the Minister to require documents from the operator, then modify, restrict or cease the activity.
  • Art. 38 — organizes inspections. It also creates an obligation to report : everyone must report to the authorities the death of fish, the destruction of their habitat or the unauthorized discharge of a harmful substance, take corrective measures and produce a report.

a) Are there really fish?

This is the first thing a government could challenge. The response to the file is massive. Scientific fishing carried out in August 2016 in the site's ditches — using electricity and fishing rods — captured 155 specimens of 9 species: mudfish (108), redfin shiner (17), fivespine stickleback (14), pumpkinseed, white sucker, pearl mullet, yellow-bellied darter, fathead minnow.

Better: it is the study of the promoter himself which recognizes that “the fish habitat is considered potential over the entire length of the watercourse studied”. She specifies that “all of the streams in the study area are in fact ditches […] and the water in the ditches comes from wetlands present on the outskirts. It flows slowly towards natural streams located outside the study area.” Some of these ditches include mature aquatic grass beds and beaver reservoirs reaching 2 meters deep. A 2020 government inspection report confirms a network of interconnected ditches actively draining eastward.

Logical conclusion of the file : it is not an isolated hole in the middle of a field. It is a connected hydraulic system, which leads to Lockhead Creek, then to Fauvel Lakes. Anything that falls in travels.

Allégation 4 — présentée comme subsidiaire

Toxic substances and the right to a healthy environment

Loi canadienne sur la protection de l'environnement (1999), art. 2(1)a.2) et a.3), 64, 68, 71, 90, 91, 93

A major legal novelty, still little known. Since the Right to a Healthy Environment Act, assented to on June 13, 2023, CEPA requires the federal government to “protect the right of every individual in Canada to a healthy environment […] subject to reasonable limits”, respecting the principles of non-regression, intergenerational equity and environmental justice — which includes the prevention of harmful effects disproportionately affecting vulnerable populations.

EN CLAIR

Since 2023, there has been a legal right to a healthy environment in Canada. And this right must be applied with future generations in mind. An adolescent who invokes this right for himself and for those who come after him is therefore not using a figure of speech: he is relying on a brand new legal text, which no one has yet really tested. This is, legally, the most innovative aspect of the case.

What is being said :

  • Between 2019 and 2024, the site would have received 120,837 tonnes of soil from the Orrington site (Maine, United States), linked to the Mallinckrodt company. 84% of this volume — 101,951 tonnes — would be classified as hazardous residue due to its mercury content. Mercury is a very volatile metal.
  • These operations take place 370 to 400 meters from residential and commercial areas.
  • The company's release inventory lists fine particles PM2.5, hexavalent chromium (recognized carcinogen), mercury, arsenic, nickel, crystalline silica and ammonia. An Englobe report would confirm the physical presence of hexavalent chromium and crystalline silica in the dust from the site.
  • The framework for implementing the right to a healthy environment, required by section 5.1(1) of CEPA, was due no later than June 13, 2025.

The criticism: ECCC would not have exercised any of its powers of evaluation (art. 68), collection of information (art. 71) or risk management (art. 90 and 91) with regard to this site.

Articulation with commercial (art. 24.4) and constitutional (art. 35) law. The Loi 93 as a lock. This is the most political argument. When a provincial government uses its parliament to neutralize local counter-powers and close legal recourse, the author maintains, the application of federal laws becomes “the one and only bulwark”. Ottawa's inaction would then create “a total legal vacuum”. The “discretion/absence of decision” distinction. This is the finest reasoning in the file. Article 24.4(2) of ACEUM explicitly recognizes that a country retains the right to exercise discretion in matters of investigation and allocation of resources; acting reasonably in exercising this discretion is not a violation. The author concedes this from the outset - then turns the argument around : we can only speak of a discretionary choice if a power has first been exercised. Here, there would be no inspection, no refusal, no reasoned decision. So not misused discretion, but a pure and simple absence of exercise. The argument a fortiori on the precedents of the CCE. In the historic case SEM-97-005, Canada was the subject of a factual case for failure to apply the Fisheries Act (discharge of acids into the St. Lawrence). In addition to the strict legislative texts, the 102-page analytical annex mobilizes several pillars of jurisprudence and general principles of Canadian law:

  • R. v. Sault Ste. Marie, [1978] 2 R.C.S. 1299 : Principle judgment establishing strict liability in environmental regulatory matters (the absence of guilty intent does not exempt from due diligence).
  • R. v. Cadillac Fairview Corp. , 2013 ONCJ 94 : Confirms that the prohibition in article 32 of LEP fully applies to unintentional harm resulting from industrial or commercial activities.
  • Reference re Impact Assessment Act, 2023 SCC 23 : Scope of residual federal constitutional jurisdiction over the protection of fisheries, birds and species at risk.
  • Imperial affair : Principle according to which the historical polluter remains responsible for its contaminants even after the transfer of land (invoked in the file for the liabilities of Camp Bouchard).
  • Sections 97 to 101 of SARA : Penal provisions providing for fines of up to $1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years for serious offenses.
  • Article 6 of SARA & Precautionary Principle : The fundamental purpose of the law requires preventing the extinction of wild species without waiting for absolute scientific certainty to be established.

Part 10 The Questions Everyone Asks

Can a minor really file an international complaint? Yes. The treaty speaks of “any person of a Party” : no age requirement. A tasty paradox: he cannot go before a court in his own country without legal representation, but he can refer the matter to a trinational commission. Moreover, it is precisely this internal impossibility which allowed it to satisfy the criterion of private remedies.

Why target Ottawa and not Quebec, when it is Quebec that authorized everything? Two reasons. First, the legal : the SEM mechanism is only aimed at central governments — Quebec literally cannot be targeted. Then, the strategic: the Loi 93 closed the provincial appeals, and two courts confirmed it in April 2025. Only the federal one remained.

How can a federal law apply to private land in Quebec? By fish and birds. Fish and their habitat, as well as migratory birds, fall under federal jurisdiction across the country, regardless of who owns the land. Section 36(3) of the Fisheries Act prohibits the discharge of a deleterious substance into waters frequented by fish “or to any other place” from which it may arrive — a ditch, for example. This is what makes this article so powerful.

If the factual record is published, what is its real impact? Its institutional, scientific and political impact is major. The factual record is an independent international investigation led by the three governments of North America. It allows neutral experts to be commissioned, the law enforcement practices of federal departments to be examined, and the public factual and scientific truth to be set in stone. In several historical precedents (such as the SEM-97-005 or North Atlantic Right Whale affair), these reports have forced administrative reforms, fueled parliamentary work and served as key evidence in the public space.

Has this mechanism already worked? He produced published factual files, including against Canada. The most famous in this context is SEM-97-005, on acid discharges in the St. Lawrence : the investigators appointed by the Secretariat concluded that Environment Canada had modified an inspection report to the point that the signing official declared that he no longer recognized it. The Secretariat has also just published, in December 2025, a factual file on the North Atlantic right whale (SEM-21-003), and in August 2025 another on the Gulf of California porpoise.

How long can the procedure last given the progress of the work? Submitted in May 2026, the process is moving through its stages at a sustained pace : following the submission of Canada's response (72-RSP) on October 16, 2026 and the citizen's reply of October 17, 2026, the Secretariat has 60 working days (art. 24.28(1) ACEUM) to recommend a factual file, while federal ministers are legally required to respond to BVG Petition No. 0546 by November 10, 2026. If the construction machinery operates in the short term, the SEM mechanism establishes federal responsibility over time: each destruction of habitat or release of contaminants occurring during Ottawa's inaction increases the factual toll for which the government will have to answer to its ACEUM partners.

What is the value and scope of the file filed by William Guindon? The legal result is exceptional : while the vast majority of citizen communications are rejected at the preliminary stage of admissibility by the Secretariat of the CCE, that of William Guindon successfully passed all the admissibility criteria of articles 24.27(1), (2) and (3) of the ACEUM. The Secretariat has validated the probative force of its corpus of 222 documentary pieces (university hydrogeological studies, wildlife characterizations, accredited laboratory analyzes of cadmium exceedances, ministerial inspection records). It is a tour de force all the more remarkable because it was accomplished in a completely autonomous and voluntary manner by a 15-year-old citizen, reaching where no municipality or any Quebec legal action had succeeded in forcing the State to be publicly accountable.

The CCE Secretariat is not a court of first instance, but a higher international institution of transparency and control of law enforcement. The major strength of the SEM process lies in its unique capacity to establish an incontestable public documentary truth, instructed by independent experts, forcing the State to answer for its actions before its trinational partners of the ACEUM and international public opinion.

  • Faced with the exceptional richness of the evidentiary corpus submitted by William Guindon - more than 200 scientific, technical, cartographic and legal documents - the Secretariat of the CCE proceeded to a methodical structuring of the file into ten major categories of evidentiary analysis :
  • Impact studies and ecological characterizations : Integration of Englobe reports (2020 and 2023) and Quebec government inventories documenting protected species and their habitats.
  • Independent hydrochemical campaigns : Analysis of WaterShed Monitoring data highlighting excesses in heavy metals (notably cadmium, arsenic, lead).
  • Ministerial inspections and compliance : Taking into account provincial inspection reports noting encroachment on the riparian strip and vegetation management.
  • Failure to issue federal authorizations : Exploitation of requests for access to information demonstrating the inaction of the Canadian federal apparatus.

Field verifications and visual documentation: Photographic records documenting episodes of aquatic mortality and sediment transfers during rainy weather.

This rigorous educational approach made it possible to validate the admissibility of the communication on all of the fundamental laws, establishing a historical precedent for a citizen initiative led by a 15-year-old author.
