[Caption] PHOTO PATRICK SANFAÇON, LA PRESSE William Guindon, 15 years old, author of the
international citizen communication SEM-26-003 filed with the Commission de coopération
environnementale (CCE / ACEUM).

William Guindon, born August 3, 2011 in Sainte-Marthe-sur-le-Lac, is a Quebec environmental
activist. He is the author of citizen communication SEM-26-003, filed on May 1, 2026 as part of the
Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement (ACEUM), aimed at curbing the expansion of the Stablex
hazardous waste landfill in Grande Tourbière de Blainville (Great Blainville Peat Bog). He was
fourteen years old at the time of filing. He has fifteen since August 3, 2026.

This text was first published on July 4, 2026, when the file was taking off. Since then, almost
everything has changed: Canada responded, the author replied, an unprecedented certificate was
issued, a federal petition was cataloged, a public register delivered its first lines, and Quebec
changed government.

Here's what happened — and what it changes in the picture.

## What has happened since July

### October 16: Ottawa responds without responding
On October 16, 2026, the deadline set by the CCE (Commission for Environmental Cooperation)
Secretariat, the Government of Canada files its official response, Exhibit 72-RSP. The Secretariat
acknowledges receipt the same day, document 73-REC, under the signature of its director of legal
affairs, Paolo Solano.

The federal brief is two pages long.

It does not contain any challenge to the material findings of the file: neither the cadmium
discharges measured up to 320 times the criterion for the protection of aquatic life, nor the
destruction of 278,000 square meters of wetlands, nor the absence of any prior federal
authorization. It does not report any enforcement measures of federal law relating to cell 6 of the
Stablex site.

Its strategy lies in a procedural dismissal. Canada requests the Secretariat to terminate
consideration of the communication, or to suspend its processing, invoking Article 24.27(4)(a) of
the ACEUM (Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement) and Guideline 7.5. In his eyes, environmental
petition No. 0546 filed with the Office of the Auditor General of Canada would be an internal
procedure still pending, which would make citizen communication premature.

In other words: the State invokes its own administrative deadlines to request the closure of a file
which documents its inaction.

### October 17: Seven means in twenty-four hours
The next day, William Guindon submitted a memorandum of observations to the Secretariat which
refuted Ottawa's request point by point. Seven arguments, based on the jurisprudence of the
mechanism.
- First means: a petition to the Auditor General is neither a judicial procedure, nor an
-   administrative procedure initiated by the Party, nor a quasi-judicial procedure. The Office of the
-   Auditor General has no sanctioning powers and does not verify the accuracy of ministerial
-   responses. It cannot therefore constitute the domestic remedy which Article 24.27(4)(a) requires
-   to be exhausted.
- Second ground: Guideline 7.5 exists to protect the citizen's recourse against external interference,
-   not to provide an escape route for the state in question.
- Third ground: the Secretariat had already ruled on this question. Paragraphs 66 to 79 of the August
-   17 decision expressly address the coexistence of approaches, relying on the Tijuana, Nazas and
-   Ship Pollution jurisprudence. Going back on it would constitute an unjustified reversal.
- Fourth ground: the two approaches have neither the same nature nor the same scope. The review before
-   the CCE concerns the application of seventeen provisions from five federal environmental laws —
-   the Migratory Birds Convention Act, the Migratory Birds Regulations, the Species at Risk Act, the
-   Fisheries Act and the Canadian Environmental Protection Act. Petition 0546 only concerns two laws.
- Fifth means: requiring the prior exhaustion of simple questions of ministerial information would
-   create a dangerous precedent for all citizens of the three signatory countries of the trade
-   agreement.
- Sixth ground: the requested postponement is by nature indeterminate, since article 24.28 of the
-   ACEUM does not provide for any reopening mechanism once an examination has been suspended.
- Seventh ground: on the merits, Canada's response does not mention any concrete measure of
-   application of federal environmental law on the site of cell 6. The alleged lack of application is
-   therefore not contested.

Conclusion requested from the Secretariat: reject the request to terminate the examination, reject
the request for suspension, and continue the investigation with a view to recommending to the
Council of Ministers of Canada, the United States and Mexico the development of a complete factual
record.

The clarification deserves to be cited as is. The CCE does not compile the age of the authors: it

## Who is William Guindon

William Guindon spent his childhood in Sainte-Marthe-sur-le-Lac, in the Laurentides region of
Quebec. He completed his primary studies in Sainte-Marthe-sur-le-Lac, a neighboring municipality.
From the sixth grade, he showed a keen interest in science and the environment by winning third
place in the regional final of the Expo-Sciences Laurentides.

He then continued his secondary school career at Externat Sacré-Cœur, a private establishment
located in Rosemère. His skills and involvement allow him to distinguish himself on several
occasions. In 2023, he received the Méritas Prize in the “ambassador — intellectual curiosity”
category, followed in 2024 by the Méritas Prize “Engagement and humanism”. His excellent academic
results also allowed him to obtain an exemption from the French and science final exams. Alongside
his studies, he got involved in the Royal Canadian Army Cadets, where he reached the silver star
level.

His involvement extends well beyond the academic framework. From the age of ten, he chose to
volunteer in his community. From June to September 2022, then again from June 2023, he offers his
time to the leisure teams of two residential and long-term care centers (CHSLD) in the Laval region.
Between 2023 and 2024, he will coordinate the community garden of the Externat Sacré-Cœur, carried
out in close collaboration with the City of Rosemère.

## A journey marked by brilliant actions

### 2021: First public attention
On November 28, 2021, at the age of ten, William Guindon attracted media attention by becoming one
of the very first Quebec children in the 5 to 11 age group to receive the COVID-19 vaccine. This
symbolic and civic gesture earned him public congratulations from the Prime Minister of Quebec,

### 2025: Political questioning in Le Devoir
A few years later, his fight shifted to political and environmental terrain. In 2025, he published
an open letter in the daily Le Devoir, entitled “François Legault, you are destroying our future”.
He directly challenges the head of the provincial government on the environmental and
intergenerational consequences resulting from the Stablex expansion project.

This letter appears the day after the adoption under gag order of Projet de loi 93 (Bill 93).

### May 1, 2026: The international legal offensive
On May 1, 2026, William Guindon took a major step forward by bringing the issue to the international
stage. He files an official communication, registered under the reference SEM-26-003, with the
Commission de coopération environnementale (Commission for Environmental Cooperation), the
monitoring body created by the ACEUM trade agreement.

In this document, he argues that Canada is failing in its international legal obligations regarding
the protection of migratory birds, species at risk and the quality of surface waters. The cause of
this failure: the authorization to expand the Stablex industrial site in the heart of a sensitive
ecological zone.

The CCE acknowledges receipt of the complaint on May 4, 2026 and begins an in-depth examination of
the file under the articles of the cross-border agreement.

On June 3, 2026, the Secretariat concluded that the communication did not yet meet all the criteria,
but invited the author to submit a revised communication. This is what he will do, six weeks later.

### May 2026: The urgent appeal to the United Nations
At the same time as his approach to the CCE, he contacted the United Nations Special Rapporteur on
toxic substances and human rights, Marcos Orellana.

In this appeal, he documents the environmental and health risk represented by the burial of
dangerous materials in a humid environment, emphasizing that this type of project infringes on the
rights of present and future generations.

### July 2026: Intervention at the Blainville municipal council
On July 2, 2026, William Guindon spoke publicly during the Blainville municipal council meeting.

He confronts Mayor Liza Poulin about the ongoing procedures and the City's transparency in this
matter.

He officially filed several key documents, including his complaint to the CCE and the United
Nations, as well as an official letter addressed to the Federal Minister of Foreign Affairs, Mélanie

On July 7, he sent a formal notice to the mayor and members of the municipal council, demanding the

The City complied: on July 9, it published on its official website a public notice confirming the

### July 2026: The alliance with the Longhouse
A few days later, he obtained institutional support of a different nature: Longhouse Stuart Myiow,

This alliance links modern legal approach to the age-old protection of the territory by indigenous

### July 16, 2026: The revised submission
On July 16, 2026, William Guindon submitted his revised communication to the Secretariat of the CCE.

This is no longer a teenage complaint. It is a 45-page legal brief, structured in six sections,
accompanied by 222 evidentiary documents, with detailed inventories, chain of custody, forensic
metadata and article-by-article concordance.

The entire file is preserved on Zenodo, CERN's open archive, under the identifier DOI
10.5281/zenodo.21416989.

The author demonstrates how Canada fails to apply five separate federal environmental laws in
connection with the Stablex cell 6 project.

### August 1, 2026: UNESCO and Committee on the Rights of the Child
On August 1, 2026, he further broadened his international offensive by submitting a joint brief to
the UNESCO World Heritage Committee and the United Nations Committee on the Rights of the Child.

It argues that the destruction of a thousand-year-old peatland and the exposure of ecosystems to
persistent contaminants violate the rights guaranteed by the Convention on the Rights of the Child,
in particular the right to a healthy environment, health and development.

### August 17, 2026: The decision of the CCE
On August 17, 2026, the Commission de coopération environnementale Secretariat issues its decision
under Article 24.27(2) and (3) of the ACEUM, document 26-2-DET2.

The CCE concludes that the revised communication meets all admissibility criteria and that it
warrants a response from the Government of Canada.

The Secretariat formally directs Canada to provide a detailed written response by October 16, 2026.

The decision made headlines. This is the first time in the history of the trade agreement that the
complaint of a minor citizen forces a signatory state to explain itself.

## The bog before the bog

We are talking about an industrial site and a wetland. We rarely talk about what was there before.

On September 25, 2026, William Guindon published an independent documentary file which places the
Grande Tourbière in a much longer temporality: six thousand years of organic memory, the history of
the Kanien'kehá:ka, Anishinaabeg and Haudenosaunee peoples, and the water territories of the Mille
Îles River, the Makigôtekw. The file also recalls that this territory was never ceded.

It is not a cultural supplement. It is a change of legal and moral framework. A peat bog is not a
wasteland that is replaced by a technical work: it is a carbon sink that took millennia to form, a
water reservoir, a habitat and a place of memory. The BAPE said it in his own way in 2023,
describing the project as premature. The indigenous file adds a voice which, in Quebec, has long
been kept away from these decisions.

The link with the rest of the fight is not theoretical: in July 2026, the support of Longhouse
Stuart Myiow, obtained under the aegis of the two-way Wampum, is exactly in line with this
continuity. Moral support — and, if necessary, support in court.

## The Stablex file: the underside of an environmental confrontation

The Grande Tourbière de Blainville is a unique millennial ecosystem in Quebec. The Stablex Canada
site has been in operation there since 1983. It is the only industrial center authorized in the
province for the final burial of inorganic industrial residues previously stabilized and solidified.

The conflict escalated in March 2025, when the Quebec government chose to adopt Projet de loi 93
under an accelerated gag procedure. This law forces the municipality of Blainville to transfer land
of great ecological value in order to allow the development of the company's new landfill zone,
called “cell 6”.

This political decision was imposed despite a very unfavorable opinion from Bureau d'audiences
publiques sur l'environnement (BAPE), published in September 2023 (Report 371), which then described
the expansion project as “premature”. To protect the company, the law adopted by Quebec includes
strict clauses excluding almost any possibility of citizen legal recourse on the merits of the case.
Legal organizations, such as the Centre québécois du droit de l'environnement (CQDE), denounce this
measure which they consider to be a serious attack on the right to an effective legal remedy.

Work on fitting out the cell has begun. The operating permit had still not been issued in the summer
of 2026: the Ministry of the Environment then indicated that the authorization request was “still
under analysis”. The matter is now the responsibility of a new government.

Citizen mobilization is also based on environmental data. Water tests conducted by citizen groups in
December 2023 reveal concentrations of cadmium in nearby surface waters up to 320 times higher than
current environmental standards. A second independent campaign, conducted in June 2026 by Climat
Québec and the Blainville Citizens' Coalition and supervised by limnologist Sonja Behmel, of
WaterShed Monitoring, also documents the presence of zinc, copper, arsenic and chromium around the
site. Stablex contests these conclusions and maintains that its most recent ministerial sampling
campaigns, published in February 2026, conclude that its activities are compliant.

Finally, opponents point out that nearly 41% of the hazardous materials buried in Blainville come
from outside Quebec, including approximately 29% directly from the United States.

## An investigation into the Registry of Lobbyists

On October 24, 2026, William Guindon published a public file constructed from a single source: the
Registry of Lobbyists of Canada. No revelation, no confidential document. Registrations, numbers,
dates, verifiable by anyone.

The register contains a registration opened on March 26, 2026, number 964373-385380, version 1.
Type: consultant lobbyist. Client: Stablex, 760 boulevard Industriel, in Blainville, represented by
its general manager, Michel Perron. Lobbyist: Alexandra Boudreault, of Catapulte communication, with
no prior public office declared. Reported parent company: Republic Services, Massena, New York. The
declared purpose, quoted in full: “Advise and inform civil servants and elected officials on the
management of residual hazardous materials at Stablex at the federal level, in addition to keeping
them informed of the work on cell 6.” Institutions targeted: Environment and Climate Change Canada
and the House of Commons.

The register also contains a monthly communication report, number 385380-701620. Communication:
September 17, 2026. Posting: September 17, 2026, the same day.

The designated public office holders met that day were three officials from Environment and Climate
Change Canada: Michael Bonser, assistant deputy minister of the International Affairs Branch; Ailish
Campbell, Senior Assistant Deputy Minister; and Mylène Sauvé, Director of Multilateral Affairs.

This is where the wires cross. Michael Bonser is not just the assistant deputy minister of a branch
of ECCC. He is the official representative of Canada to the Commission de coopération
environnementale: the one whose name appears on the liaison documents with the Secretariat, the one
whose services coordinated the drafting of the official response due on October 16.

A month separates the September 17 meeting from Ottawa's October 16 response. The two facts are in
separate public records. One at the Office of the Commissioner of Lobbying of Canada, the other at
the communications registry of the CCE. The file published on October 24 simply puts them side by
side.

The register finally shows a formal anomaly, which the file is content to document. For this
specific registration, the Registry of Lobbyists of Canada only displays one monthly communication
report — that of September 17 — while the “Reporting period” field displays “August 2026”. A
communication held in September declared over a period of August: a typo of entry or an
administrative catch-up, but a trace that the register keeps as is.

You have to be precise about what this file says and does not say. He cites public records. It
assumes no intention, alleges no collusion and asserts no causal link. The Register of Lobbyists is
a register of transparency: lobbyists declare their activities there because the law obliges them to
do so, and the fact of declaring a meeting does not constitute an offense. What the file shows is
the temporal concomitance between a lobbying activity targeting the very management that coordinated
Canada's response and the filing of a procedural dismissal four weeks later.

## A precedent for minors

This issue is not only environmental. It raises a legal question that goes beyond Grande Tourbière:
that of direct access for minors to international justice.

On September 23, 2026, William Guindon published a plea on the legal autonomy of adolescents in the
face of the structural adultism of Quebec and Canadian law. Its central argument: biological age
cannot be an automatic inadmissibility criterion when a citizen demonstrates his capacity for
discernment and provides rigorous proof.

On October 14, 2026, in Berlin, this plea received scientific support. Manfred Liebel, professor
emeritus at the Technical University of Berlin and global figure for children's rights, signs a

The practical significance is there: a file of two hundred and twenty-two evidentiary documents,
filed without a lawyer, which forces the federal government of Canada to mobilize its legal teams
and produce an official response in an international treaty. If a fifteen-year-old can do it, the
age barrier is no longer a procedural inevitability.

It is also in this light that the rest of the work must be read: the full-text search engine and the

## Democratizing environmental justice

Making your voice heard shouldn't be an obstacle course.

After having passed the procedural requirements of the Commission de coopération environnementale,
the young activist refuses to allow his apprenticeship to remain an isolated experience.

Refusing to remain a simple observer, William Guindon launched the writing of a practical and
popularized guide for citizens and young people wishing to understand the workings of international
law and navigate complex legal processes.

Objectives: decode the legislative framework and Canadian and international environmental
regulations, popularize the steps necessary to submit an admissible communication, and share the
lessons learned from one's own journey with ministries and the courts.
