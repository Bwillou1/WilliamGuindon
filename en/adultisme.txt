## 2. Conceptual insight: From ageism to structural adultism

In the media space and everyday language, any discrimination based on age is often described as
“ageism”. In contemporary sociological research on children's rights, this term is insufficient:

#### Why “ageism” is reductive
The word “ageism” refers to interpersonal stereotypes or aging. It obscures the political,
institutional and legal dimension of the subordination of young people.

#### Adultism
Defined by Liebel and Meade (2026), adultism designates a relationship of systemic domination by
which adults arrogate discretionary power over minors and systematically presume their civic
incapacity.

#### Structural adultism in administrative law
It is expressed when administrations establish the civil age as an end of inadmissibility: refusal
of access to public documents, refusal to respond on the technical merits, or infantilization during
public hearings.

#### Concrete Agency
The ability demonstrated in the field by a young citizen to investigate, analyze complex technical
data and obtain a historic decision before the CCE (ACEUM) shatters the theoretical presumption of
incompetence.

## 3. Petition e-7834 to the House of Commons of Canada

### Petition e-7834 to the House of Commons assembled in Parliament

#### Official text submitted to the House of Commons:
- Whereas section 15(1) of the Canadian Charter guarantees everyone the same protection and equal
-   benefit of the law, without discrimination based on age;
- Whereas article 12 of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, ratified by Canada, requires that
-   the opinion of the discerning child be heard and considered according to his age and maturity in
-   administrative procedures;
- Whereas the Code civil du Québec (Civil Code of Québec) allows minors aged 14 and over to consent
-   alone to certain care, under the conditions of articles 14 and 17;
- Whereas Canada currently has no federal legal framework or independent commissioner to protect young
-   people against administrative and institutional paternalism;
- Whereas automatically presuming the incapacity of young people aged 14 to 17 in their civic and
-   administrative activities contravenes their intellectual integrity and may constitute
-   discrimination.
- To adopt, within its areas of jurisdiction, a framework law against ageism and paternalism in
-   services and institutions, public or private, under federal jurisdiction, and to negotiate a
-   pan-Canadian agreement;
- To create an independent Federal Youth Commissioner (ombudsman), accessible to young people,
-   responsible for receiving complaints and investigating federal institutions;
- To establish, for young people aged 14 to 17, an automatic and rebuttable presumption of capacity to
-   participate in administrative decisions concerning them, reversible only after an individualized
-   assessment of their age, maturity and competence, never on age alone, with written reasons,
-   respect for autonomy and the right to be heard.

#### Test of the facts: Chronology of sponsorship processes (October 2026)
On the official electronic petition portal of the House of Commons (NosCommunes), a citizen petition
must be authorized by a Member of Parliament before being published and open to public signatures.
Without this formal sponsorship, the procedure remains blocked. In accordance with parliamentary
regulations, each elected official has discretionary sponsorship power. The chronology below records
in a strictly factual manner the exchanges that took place with the office of Ms. Anna Gainey,
Federal Member of Parliament for Notre-Dame-de-Grâce—Westmount and Secretary of State for Children
and Youth of the Government of Canada.

Transmission by William Guindon of an official request to the MP's parliamentary address ([email
protected]): “Electronic petition e-7834 — Fight against ageism and recognition of the capacity of
young people”. Presentation of the approach and request for sponsorship to authorize publication on
the NosCommunes portal.

Receipt of generic automatic acknowledgments of receipt from the parliamentary office, without
personalized support or response on the merits of the request.

Transmission of an urgent request indicating the procedural deadline for publication on NosCommunes
(October 16), with a certified copy to the office of the Secretary of State for Children and Youth.

Follow-up email: “Petition e-7834 blocked on NosCommunes — request for intervention from your
office”. New automatic response generated by the desktop, without background processing.

Several citizens are writing directly to the MP's parliamentary office to support William Guindon's
approach and request authorization to sponsor petition e-7834.

The office notifies its refusal to follow up on the sponsorship request. William Guindon formally
acknowledges this in writing. A personal courtesy response is sent by the office (“Thank you for
contacting us… we are always happy to receive messages from young Canadians”), confirming the
absence of sponsorship and the absence of authorization for publication on the parliamentary portal.

Despite the sending of citizen support, it was noted that emails were classified as junk mail
(“spam”) and that no sponsorship follow-up was granted by the office.

The official House of Commons petition system formally records: "Refused by Anna Gainey on October
31, 2026 at 7:18 p.m. (ET)". The Member of Parliament for Notre-Dame-de-Grâce—Westmount and
Secretary of State for Children and Youth officially declines the sponsorship required for the
publication of petition e-7834.

In accordance with the procedure of the House of Commons, the request for authorization to publish
petition e-7834 is immediately redirected and transmitted to another federal Member in order to seek
the parliamentary sponsorship necessary to open it for public signatures.

#### Democratic scope: the documentary demonstration of structural adultism
This situation concretely illustrates the thesis developed by Prof. Dr Manfred Liebel and Philip
Meade: young people aged 14 to 17, not having the right to vote, do not have any autonomous
institutional lever to submit their proposals to public debate. Even when a young citizen prepares a
documented petition consistent with section 15 of the Canadian Charter and section 12 of the
Convention on the Rights of the Child, his or her approach depends entirely on the discretionary
goodwill of an elected adult. The gap between the mission of Secretary of State for Youth and the
refusal to authorize the publication of the petition demonstrates why the formal recognition of a
presumption of civic capacity and the creation of an independent Federal Youth Commissioner are
essential.

Electronic petition e-7834 remains registered in the registry of the House of Commons and can be
sponsored by any federal MP sitting in Parliament. If you wish to support democratic opening to
14-17 year olds, you can contact your own constituency MP to ask them to sponsor and authorize the
publication of petition e-7834 on NosCommunes.ca.
