Collective Citizen Video (90 seconds) — Great Peat Bog of Blainville

“We are creating a short 90-second collective video where each citizen reads one sentence asking the ministers of Canada, the United States, and Mexico to agree to open an independent scientific Factual Record on the Great Peat Bog of Blainville.”

1. Why are we making this video?

Show that William is not alone

The Government of Canada moved to dismiss or defer the submission on October 16. Showing 25 to 50 citizens of all ages standing behind him proves that SEM-26-003 is not an isolated teenager's initiative, but the legitimate demand of an entire community.

Maintain public oversight before the decision

Publicly remind all three USMCA governments (Canada, the United States, Mexico) that citizens are closely watching the process ahead of the Article 24.28 vote.

Engage journalists and allies

Reporters rarely cover a 200-page legal filing, but they share a moving collective video uniting citizens—from children to grandparents—ahead of the holidays.

3. Quick filming tips (30 seconds)

  • Length: 1 sentence per person (roughly 3 to 5 seconds).
  • Framing & light: face a window or well-lit space, keep your phone steady at eye level.
  • Audio: record in a quiet room without wind or background TV, and speak clearly.
  • Margin: pause 1 second before speaking and 1 second after finishing for clean editing.

4. The 27 sentences of the collective script (90 seconds)

Pick any available sentence from 2 to 26 (sentences 1 and 27 are reserved for William Guindon’s opening and closing). Taken sentences are crossed out below. Click Copy to keep it handy while recording.

1

(William Guindon): Six thousand years ago—long before the pyramids, before writing, before the word “border”—an ombrotrophic peatland was born on Canadian soil.

2

Since then, layer after layer, it has silently stored sixty centuries of carbon.

3

Every spring, after migrating from Mexico across the United States, thousands of birds return to nest in its mosses.

4

Your three countries, gathered around the USMCA table, are already linked by the flight of these birds.

5

Today, we are citizens of all ages and backgrounds standing up to speak to you about that bond.

6

We have no armies. We have no lobbyists, nor the budgets of multinational corporations.

7

But we have numbers, and we have time—entire generations ahead of us to remember.

8

On July 28, 2022, your three governments voted at the United Nations to recognize the human right to a clean, healthy, and sustainable environment.

9

That right is ours. And it belongs to unborn children—the only citizens of Earth with no lawyer and no seat in your room.

10

You created a rare tool in your treaty: the right for an ordinary citizen to say, “verify whether our environmental laws are being enforced.”

11

One of us, a fifteen-year-old Canadian citizen, used it by filing international citizen submission SEM-26-003.

12

This submission demonstrates, with supporting evidence, that laws protecting our migratory birds, species at risk, and wetlands are not being enforced.

13

Why speak to you about a peatland in Canada? Because the promise itself is continental.

14

Because the treaty bears your signature. And because a “yes” to the truth reads the same in every language.

15

We do not ask you to take our word for it.

16

We do not ask you to assign blame or decide the outcome today.

17

We ask only one thing: let the facts be established.

18

In the vote under Article 24.28 of the USMCA, you will decide whether a public, independent Factual Record is prepared.

19

The equation is simple: if this young citizen is wrong, the investigation will say so, and the file will close.

20

But if he is right, the entire world has the right to know.

21

Either way, the truth wins. There is only one losing vote: a vote against the facts.

22

More than thirty years ago, in Rio, the world applauded speeches on the environment before looking away.

23

This time is different: there is a binding treaty, an official vote, and your three names at the bottom of the page.

24

One day, your own children and grandchildren will ask you: “On the day you were asked to open your eyes, how did you vote?”

25

Write an answer you will be proud of.

26

Each for one place. All for the same Earth.

27

(William Guindon): The Great Peat Bog has waited six thousand years. It can wait for your vote.
